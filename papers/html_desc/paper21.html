Papers XV–XX identify the Standard-Model matter sectors with finite subgroups of the binary icosahedral vacuum group 2I — the neutrino (QH=1, A-type), the charged lepton (QH=2, 2I itself, E8), and the quark/baryon (QH=3, binary tetrahedral 2T, E6). Paper XXI reads that ladder as an ADE classification and asks what it forces at the top of the tier and off to the side, then develops — honestly and conditionally — the dark-matter consequence of the sideways branch. Two structural results are firm group theory and rational-CFT arithmetic; the dark-matter application is a developed, falsifiable, but conditional scenario, not a derivation of ΩDM.

  • ADE completion and the exclusion of E7 matter Because the matter sectors are subgroups of the vacuum group 2I (order 120), not free McKay labels, the binary octahedral group 2O (McKay E7, order 48) can carry no matter sector: 48 does not divide 120, so Lagrange's theorem forbids 2O ⊂ 2I (equivalently S4 is not a subgroup of A5). The Hopfion ladder admits A-type, E6, and E8 matter but never E7 — a definite, falsifiable prediction independent of everything below.
  • The D-type slot is available, colourless, and non-baryonic The binary dihedral (dicyclic) groups 2D3 (order 12, D̂5) and 2D5 (order 20, D̂7) lie in 2I but not in 2T (A4 has no subgroup of order 6, so neither dicyclic lifts into the quark group). They are ‘sideways branches’ of the vacuum that carry their own ℤ2 outer automorphism rather than the colour ℤ3: colourless, non-baryonic, and reserved for beyond–Standard-Model physics. These are the candidate dark sectors.
  • The D-type sector is sterile: no T-matrix closure A sector carries a quantised charge Qgroup only if its WZW T-matrix closes (Q Tj an odd multiple of ¼ for every primary). The Standard-Model sectors close — (E6)1→3, SU(2)1→6, SU(2)3→10 — but neither natural D-type assignment admits any closing integer: for SO(10)1 and SO(14)1 the spinor and vacuum phases demand opposite parities of Q r, and every even-level SU(2)k (k=6, 10 for the two candidates) fails. With no Qgroup and no ℤ3 to support an SU(3)L representation, the sector carries no electric and no weak charge: a full Standard-Model singlet, interacting only gravitationally and through its own ℤ2.
  • The same spinor makes the sector Dirac The obstruction to closure is the odd-rank spinor hs=r/8, and odd rank is exactly the condition (r=5 for SO(10), r=7 for SO(14)) under which the SO(2r) spinor is complex (s* = c≠s) — the same fact that makes the SO(10) 16 complex and chiral. One object therefore renders the sector both sterile (no quantised charge) and Dirac (a conserved dark-number U(1), hence a distinct antiparticle and an admissible asymmetry), in contrast to the self-conjugate, Majorana neutrino sector, which can carry no asymmetry.
  • Coldness and an a−3 equation of state, conditionally A sterile gravitating sector is dark-matter-viable if it is cold at recombination. If its gravitating content is a population of localised, number-conserved coherent configurations of fixed size and energy, the energy density dilutes as a−3 (w=0, cold); if instead it relaxes to a smooth space-filling texture, gradients give ρ ∝ a−2 (w=−⅓, not cold). Which obtains is set by the inertial director dynamics: coarsening slows as the signal speed cdir→0, with no static plateau but a coarsening time that diverges, and repeated mergers re-seed and sustain the localised network — so the a−3 regime holds below the same coldness threshold the director sector already requires, a genuine escape from the a−2 obstruction at no new cost.
  • Abundance from asymmetric-dark-matter co-genesis The CP phase δCP is a property of the shared 2I vacuum, so one chiral, CP-violating reconnection biases both the 2T (baryon) and 2D3 (dark) freeze-outs, seeding correlated asymmetries; the symmetric dark part self-annihilates through the shared director field, leaving the Dirac asymmetric residue. With ΩDM/Ωb=r (mdark/mp) and the gravitating mass per relic its full rest mass (a stable knot loses none to a residue), r ≈ 1 reproduces the observed ΩDM/Ωb ≈ 5.4 at mdark ≈ 5 GeV — the canonical asymmetric-dark-matter scale, a sterile state evading direct detection by construction. This fixes mdark given r; it is a conditional consistency, not a parameter-free prediction.

The falsifiable signatures are the firm no-E7 prediction, a ∼5 GeV sterile asymmetric relic, self-interacting (cored-halo) dark matter through the shared director field, and a merger-dependent mass excess shared with Paper VII's cluster channel. The branching ratio is now estimated (not assumed): the shared freeze-out weights each sector by its thermal degrees of freedom, the WZW central charge, giving r ≈ c(SO(10)1)/c((E6)1) = 5/6 ≈ 0.83. The sector's stability is likewise now fully argued: against unwinding it is topological (Hopf-charge conservation, as for the trefoil); against decay into Standard-Model sectors it is barrier-protected (2D3, 2D5 are sideways branches, not in 2T, so decay needs a barriered T(2,2)→T(2,3) reconnection) and charge-protected by the conserved dark number — the spinor sits in the ℤ2⊂ℤ4 centre of Spin(10) carrying dark-number 1 (the Dirac complex-spinor U(1), the standard SO(10) matter parity), while the Standard-Model sectors, living in different chiral algebras, carry dark-number 0; a decay would change the dark number and is forbidden by its conservation — the same conserved charge that carries the asymmetry. Two quantities remain genuinely open: the soliton's existence as an energy minimiser (a charge-conserving relaxation is needed, as free-flow relaxation loses Hopf charge even for the known trefoil), and, via the companion Paper XXII, the magnitude of the baryon asymmetry (reduced to a single density-feedback number). Results (i)–(ii), the no-E7 exclusion and the sterile-Dirac character, stand independently of the dark-matter application.
