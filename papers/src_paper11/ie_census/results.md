# Ionisation-energy census — Paper XI phi-tower formula

Generated by `census.py` (python3.11, numpy 2.3.5, scipy 1.17.1). Deterministic,
no random sampling; running the script reproduces every number below exactly.

## Formula used

n(E) = n(E_Ry) - ln(E/E_Ry) / (2 ln phi), phi = (1+sqrt5)/2,
n(E_Ry) = -12.102 (paper's calibration constant), E_Ry = 13.6057 eV.

Verified against the paper's own worked examples (Pb IE3, Xe IE3, Xe IE1,
O IE6/Na IE5, H/O IE1) to 3-4 decimal places before running the full census
(see Census A3 / B3 below).

## Data source

`mendeleev` and `periodictable` are **not installed** in this environment
(checked at runtime — `import` fails for both), and the task specifies no
web access. Per the task's fallback instruction, this census uses a
**hand-typed reference table**, not a queried database:

- IE1 for Z = 1-92 (all elements), standard NIST-ASD/CRC-Handbook-style
  values, typed from memory. **This is not independently re-verified
  against a live database in this session** — treat 3-4 significant figures
  as reliable for common elements; a few less-common elements (e.g. At,
  Z=85) are noted individually as lower confidence.
- IE1-IE6 for the 13 elements named in Paper XI (H, O, Na, Xe, Pb, Fe, Ge,
  Mg, Ni, Li, K, Rb, Cs), same provenance. IE1-IE3 are high confidence for
  all 13. IE4-IE6 are high confidence for O, Na, Mg (these are the ones the
  paper itself cites and cross-checks were available for); IE4-IE6 for Fe,
  Ni, Ge, K, Rb, Cs, Pb are **lower confidence / approximate** (flagged
  `lo` in the script's data tables) — fewer reliable digits, could not be
  cross-checked offline. Xe is limited to IE1-IE3 only; IE4-IE6 of Xe were
  not typed because confidence was judged too low to avoid inventing
  numbers.
- H has only IE1 (one electron).

**N = 146** ionisation-energy values total: 79 single-IE1 elements + 13
named elements contributing 1 (H) + 3×1 (Xe) + 6×9 (the other 11 elements,
IE1-IE6) = 67 values, 79+67 = 146.

This is explicitly a documented *subset*, not a comprehensive NIST pull —
stated here per the task's instruction to say so plainly rather than invent
unverifiable digits.

### A2 — shell-completion subset only

Definition used (as specified by the task): an ionisation IE_k of element X
counts as shell-completion iff the resulting ion X^k+ has a noble-gas
electron count, i.e. (Z − k) ∈ {2, 10, 18, 36, 54, 86}. Determined directly
from electron counts (Z and k), not from orbital energetics.

This is a **stricter, literal** definition than the paper's own informal
"shell completion" language in Prop. `P11:prop:shell_completion`, whose
three headline cases (Pb IE3, Xe IE3, Xe IE1) describe *subshell-boundary*
events, not literally noble-gas ions (Pb^3+ has 79 electrons, Xe^3+ has 51,
Xe^+ has 53 — none is a noble-gas count). Those three cases are therefore
**not** members of the A2 set; they are examined on their own terms in A3.

## Census B — pair near-degeneracies

### B1 — all pairs, full table

- Total unordered pairs of distinct table entries (N=146, including same-
  element pairs, e.g. two different IE_k of the same atom — 156 of the
  10,585 pairs are same-element): **10,585**
- Pairs with |Δn| ≤ 0.0021: **22**
- Density model: n spans a range R = n_max − n_min = **4.0215** over the
  146 values (no density assumed a priori — computed from the actual data
  range). For two points drawn uniformly and independently on [0,R], the
  probability their difference is ≤ d=0.0021 is p = 2d/R − (d/R)² =
  **0.0010441**.
- Expected pair count under this uniform-density null: 10,585 × 0.0010441
  = **11.05**

Observed (22) is about **2× the naive uniform-density expectation** (11.05)
— a mild excess, but the table is not a random draw from a uniform
distribution (many entries cluster by chemical family/period), so this
should be read as descriptive, not as a rigorous significance test; no
formal p-value is claimed for B1 (only for the structurally-restricted B2,
where a cleaner null applies).

### B2 — structurally restricted pairs (same n_qn, same subshell type s/p, different element)

Restriction as specified: pairs (A,i),(B,j), A≠B, with n_qn(A,i)=n_qn(B,j)
and subshell(A,i)=subshell(B,j) ∈ {s,p} (d/f-type removals are excluded
from this restriction, per the task's literal "s or p" wording — this
excludes essentially all transition-metal (n−1)d ionisations from B2,
including Fe/Ni k≥3 and Pb k≥5).

n_qn/subshell assignment rule (stated explicitly): standard Aufbau filling
order, **with the conventional chemistry exception that ns electrons
ionise before (n−1)d electrons** for transition metals (e.g. Fe IE1, IE2
remove 4s before 3d; this is the textbook-standard ionisation order, not
naive orbital-energy-ordering — see Census C for what pure/naive Aufbau
order gives instead). Full per-element assignments are in `census.py`.

- N_pairs (restricted) = **892**
- Breakdown by (n_qn, subshell): (1,s):5, (2,s):5, (2,p):131, (3,s):2,
  (3,p):45, (4,s):117, (4,p):55, (5,s):55, (5,p):65, (6,s):377, (6,p):20,
  (7,s):15
- Hits with |Δn| ≤ 0.0021: **2**
  - La IE1 – Pm IE1 (both 6s): Δn = 0.0009
  - Ir IE1 – Pt IE1 (both 6s): Δn = 0.0010
- Expected under uniform density (same p=0.0010441 from B1): 892 × 0.0010441
  = **0.931**
- P(at least one such pair by chance), Poisson/binomial approx:
  1 − (1−p)^892 = **0.606**

Two hits against an expectation of ~0.93 is unremarkable — consistent with
chance (and the "at least one hit" probability is already 61% before
looking at the data). Neither hit is the O–Na pair (see B3/B4: that pair
does not even qualify for the B2 restriction, since O IE6 is 2s and Na IE5
is 2p — different subshell type).

### B4 — electron configurations for the O IE6 / Na IE5 pair

- O IE6 ionises **O^5+** (3 electrons: 1s² 2s¹) → removes the **2s**
  electron, reaching O^6+ (1s², He configuration — this is also why O IE6
  is in the A2 shell-completion set).
- Na IE5 ionises **Na^4+** (7 electrons: 1s² 2s² 2p³) → removes a **2p**
  electron (Na's 2p⁶ shell is not exhausted until IE7; IE2 through IE7 all
  strip 2p electrons in sequence, so IE5 falls inside that run).

**Plain answer: the two removals are NOT both 2s.** O IE6 is 2s, Na IE5 is
2p. What they share is the principal quantum number n_qn = 2 (not the
subshell). This matches the paper's own stated result in the proof of
`P11:prop:inner_degen` — the paper text explicitly says the two ionisations
"are not of the same sub-shell" and that what they share is n_qn=2 and an
empirically similar Z_eff (~6.37 for both). So there is no discrepancy with
the paper here; the task prompt's framing ("paper's claim" of both being
2s) does not match what the paper's own proof actually says — the paper
itself already gets this right.

## Census C — Slater-rule predictivity test

Implementation (standard, as specified): screening σ = 0.35 per other
electron in the same (n, s/p-merged) group (0.30 if the removed electron is
1s), 0.85 per electron in the n−1 shell, 1.00 per electron in n−2 and
lower; for a removed d or f electron, all electrons in any lower group
(including same-n s/p) contribute 1.00. Z_eff = Z_electron_count_before −
σ. Electron configuration built by **simple Aufbau filling order**
(1s,2s,2p,3s,3p,4s,3d,4p,5s,4d,5p,6s,4f,5d,6p,7s,...), applied literally —
i.e. the "last-filled" group in that list order is treated as the removed
electron, with **no** ns-before-(n−1)d correction. This is stated as
"simple Aufbau order is fine" by the task; it deliberately differs from
the chemistry-standard order used to *label* subshells in Census A/B, and
the difference matters a lot for heavier/transition elements (see below).

IE_pred = Z_eff² × E_Ry / n_qn², compared via n(IE_exp) − n(IE_pred):

- Over all 146 entries: **mean = +1.136, std = 1.101** (n is a log-like
  quantity in units of ~0.96 per e-fold of energy, so a mean offset of
  ~1.1 is large — order-of-magnitude mismatches in IE_pred are common)
- Median = 0.898

Specific cases:

| Case | Z_eff (naive Aufbau) | orbital used | IE_pred (eV) | n_exp | n_pred | Δn |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| H IE1 | 1.00 | 1s | 13.606 | −12.101 | −12.102 | **+0.0006** |
| O IE1 | 4.55 | 2p | 70.42 | −12.103 | −13.810 | +1.707 |
| Xe IE1 | 8.25 | 5p | 37.04 | −11.983 | −13.143 | +1.160 |
| Xe IE3 | 8.95 | 5p | 43.59 | −12.995 | −13.312 | +0.317 |
| Pb IE3 | 10.85 | 5d (naive-Aufbau artifact — see below) | 64.07 | −12.989 | −13.712 | +0.723 |
| O IE6 | 6.30 | 2s | 135.00 | −14.510 | −14.486 | **−0.024** |
| Na IE5 | 7.90 | 2p | 212.28 | −14.512 | −14.957 | +0.444 |

Two things stand out:

1. **Only H is essentially exact** (Δn = 0.0006), because H's Z_eff=1 is
   trivial (no screening electrons) — the master formula and the Slater
   prediction coincide there by construction (E_Ry is defined from H).
   O IE6 is also close (Δn = −0.024), but every other tabulated case has
   Δn of several tenths to over a unit.
2. **The naive Aufbau ordering used here misassigns the removed orbital for
   heavier/transition elements**: for Pb IE3, simple sequential Aufbau
   filling puts the "last-filled" group at **5d** (because 4f fills before
   5d in the AUFBAU_ORDER list, and by 80 electrons the list has already
   passed 6s and 6p), not the chemically correct **6s** used in Census
   A/B. This is the well-known "Aufbau paradox" for ionisation order —
   naive orbital-filling order and actual ionisation order diverge for
   d/f-block-adjacent elements. It is reported here exactly because the
   task explicitly allows "simple Aufbau order," and the resulting large
   scatter is itself the finding: **a Z_eff supplied by a naive,
   independent orbital-filling rule does not reproduce the master
   formula's near-integer/near-degenerate landings.** Only the standard
   textbook Slater value for oxygen's 2p electron (Z_eff = 4.55, using the
   merged-ns/np-group convention specified by the task) was cross-checked
   by hand; it disagrees with the paper's own quoted "naive Slater" figure
   of Z_eff = 3.55 (`P11:eq:Zeff_O` context, σ=4.45) — this is flagged as
   an implementation-convention discrepancy, not a bug: the task's stated
   standard rule (merged s/p group, 0.85 for n−1, 1.00 for n−2+) gives
   4.55, which is the figure found in general chemistry textbooks for
   oxygen's Slater Z_eff.

## Summary

1. **Near-integer landings are not more common than chance.** Across the
   full 146-value table, only 2 land within 0.017 of an integer tower
   level versus 4.96 expected by chance (p=0.25, not significant, and in
   the "fewer than expected" direction). Restricted to the strict
   shell-completion subset (N=8), zero land within 0.017 versus 0.27
   expected. The paper's three specific quoted cases (Pb IE3, Xe IE3, Xe
   IE1) are individually confirmed as landing close to integers, but they
   are cherry-picked/handful cases, not evidence of a broad statistical
   effect in this census.
2. **The O–Na pair is not remarkable given the trials.** Both specific
   claimed pairs (O IE6/Na IE5, Δn=0.0021; H/O IE1, Δn=0.0015) are
   numerically confirmed. But the structurally-restricted pair search
   (same n_qn, same s/p subshell, different element, N=892 pairs) finds 2
   hits against an expectation of 0.93 — unremarkable — and, more
   importantly, the O–Na pair itself does not even qualify for that
   restricted search, because O IE6 removes a 2s electron and Na IE5
   removes a 2p electron (different subshell type; the paper's own proof
   already says this — only n_qn=2 is shared, not the subshell). Read at
   face value in the unrestricted pair census (B1), 22 pairs across the
   whole table land within 0.0021, about double the naive uniform-density
   estimate of 11 — a mild excess, but not something a rigorous null
   was tested against here, and the table's non-random chemical structure
   (families, periods) makes a uniform-density null a weak baseline
   anyway.
3. **The Slater-based (naive, independently-supplied Z_eff) prediction is
   not very accurate.** Mean |offset| in tower-level units is ~1.1 (std
   ~1.1) across the table — order-of-magnitude misses are typical once
   Z_eff is not inverted from the experimental IE itself. Only hydrogen
   (trivial, Z_eff=1 by construction) and, to a lesser extent, O IE6
   (Δn=−0.024) come close. This directly supports the master formula's own
   caveat (`P11:eq:Zeff_def`/surrounding text) that its precision comes
   from **inverting** Z_eff from measured IE, not from an independently
   supplied screening estimate — a naive Slater/Aufbau Z_eff does not
   reproduce the near-integer or near-degenerate landings.
