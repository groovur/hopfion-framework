Paper XI asks what follows immediately downstream: if αem and me are fixed by the condensate, then so is the Rydberg energy 𝔼Ry = αem2me/2 — and therefore the entire atomic energy scale.

  • The optical window. The red edge of visible light coincides with the pure tower level Λcond φ20 = 1.799 eV to 1.7%, with no loop correction — the same level index as the electron mass.
  • Hydrogen–oxygen near-degeneracy. H and O sit within Δn = 0.0015 of each other on the spiral, both at the Rydberg level. What the framework fixes is the level itself: n(𝔼Ry) = −12.102 with no free parameters, where hydrogen sits by definition. Alkali metal reactivity with water is identified as a spiral-gap phenomenon. All five stable alkali metals sit approximately one full spiral level above the H–O cluster (n ≈ −12.10), meaning their outer electrons are bound φ2 ≈ 2.6× more weakly than water’s — making electron donation thermodynamically driven for the entire group. The gap increases monotonically Li → Cs, correctly ordering the reactivity series with no additional parameters. The transition from vigorous (Na) to explosive (K, Rb, Cs) violence correlates with melting points below 100 °C, a kinetic amplification effect outside the spiral’s scope.

The paper proposes the φ-spiral as an alternative supplemental organisational principle for the periodic table, ordered by binding energy rather than electron configuration. Chemistry is not fully derived here from the condensate, but it is calibrated by it through the two framework output quantities αem and me.
